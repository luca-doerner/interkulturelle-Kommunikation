• Setup:

  General Communication Styles


Direct Communication
• Message is stated clearly and explicitly
• Common in low-context cultures (e.g., U.S., Germany)
• Prioritizes clarity and efficiency
Example: “I disagree with your idea.”


                                    Indirect Communication
                                    • Message is implied, suggested, or hinted at
                                    • Common in high-context cultures (e.g., Japan, India, UK sometimes)
                                    • Prioritizes politeness, harmony, and context
                                    Example: “That’s an interesting idea, but maybe we could also
                                    consider…”




                    In teams: Direct styles may seem rude to indirect speakers;
                     indirect styles may seem vague to direct speakers..

   01/06/2026       Prof. Lamya Abdullah - IKHT - BINT24 - SoSe2026                                        35
 General Communication Styles

Formal Communication
• Polite, structured, and often uses titles
• Used in hierarchical or high power distance cultures (e.g.,
  Korea, France)
• Communication follows roles and status


Example: “Dear Mr. Tanaka, I hope this message finds you
well.”
                                         Informal Communication
                                         • Casual, relaxed, and may use first names
                                         • Common in low power distance cultures (e.g., Australia, Netherlands)
                                         • Communication is based on familiarity or comfort


                                         Example: “Hey Kenji! Just checking in—how’s it going?”

 In teams: Informal styles may seem disrespectful to formal cultures;
 formality may seem distant to informal cultures.

  01/06/2026       Prof. Lamya Abdullah - IKHT - BINT24 - SoSe2026                                                36
   General Communication Styles

Verbal Communication
• Face-to-face, phone, video, spoken
• Allows immediate feedback
• Emphasizes tone, body language


Common in oral-tradition or relationship-based cultures


                                         Written Communication
                                         •   Emails, memos, reports, chat
                                         •   Offers clarity, documentation, and record-keeping
                                         •   Emphasizes clarity and correctness


                                         Common in task-based, legalistic, or documentation-heavy cultures


  In teams: Some cultures prefer talking to solve problems;
  others prefer emails to avoid misinterpretation.

  General Communication Styles


High-Context Communication
• Relies on shared background, nonverbal cues
• Much is “unsaid” — meaning is in the context
• Relationship-oriented


Common in East Asia, Arab world, Latin America

                                            Low-Context Communication
                                            • Relies on explicit, detailed information
                                            • Everything important is said directly in words
                                            • Task- or result-oriented


                                            Common in Germany, Scandinavia, North America


In teams: High-context communicators may expect others to “read between the lines”;
low-context communicators may expect others to “say what they mean.”

Exercise : Paraphrase Practice



• Duration: 5 minutes
• Pair up participants.
• Instructions:
  • Person A explains a simple process or idea (e.g., how to order office supplies). [3 mins]
  • Person B listens carefully and then paraphrases what they heard in their own words. [3-5 mins]
  • Person A confirms or corrects the paraphrase.
  • Swap roles and repeat with a new idea.

    Case Study: Intercultural Team Conflict




The Setup: An Intercultural project-team consisting of members from Brazil, India, and Germany is working on a
critical software release.

The Incident: The team has just missed a major project milestone.

The Conflict:
• The German team members feel the Brazilian team is too relaxed about timelines and lacks structure.

•   The Brazilian team members feel the German team is overly rigid, and creates unnecessary friction.

•   The Indian team members are trying to stay supportive but are hesitant to voice their concerns openly to
    avoid worsening the conflict.


→ Pick one Tool SBAR or DESC, and describe/analyze the approach to resolve the issue
