Why Presentation Is a Professional Skill ?



A professional presentation is a tool for:
                                                                                               Presentation ≠ PowerPoint
• Informing : What does the audience need to know?                                                 Or Slides by AI…
• Explaining : Can I make a complex idea understandable?
• Persuading : Why should the audience agree or act?
• Aligning : Can different people leave with the same understanding?
• Building credibility : Do I appear competent and trustworthy?
• Enabling decisions : Can my presentation help people decide?




                                                                   A good presentation is not about showing what you know.
                                                                  It is about helping the audience understand what matters?

Storytelling in Intercultural Teams                                           The same story can be interpreted differently.




When presenting to a heterogeneous audience, consider:
  ➢ What examples are familiar to them?
  ➢ How much context do they need?
  ➢ What communication style is expected?
  ➢ Could the story or humor be misunderstood?
  ➢ What values or assumptions are embedded in the story?




→ Adapt the story to the audience — without reducing people to cultural stereotypes

3 Success Factors
The three Layers Model




If one fails:
• Great content + poor delivery → confusing
• Great delivery + poor content → entertaining but empty
• Great slides + poor explanation → presentation becomes "reading slides"
• Great presentation + wrong audience adaptation → technically correct but ineffective

  Handling Questions


  Questions Are natural Part of the Presentation
  →should not interpret every question as criticism.


  A question may mean:
    ➢ genuine curiosity
    ➢ disagreement                                                      Example:
    ➢ clarification                                                         ➢ "That's an important point."
    ➢ testing your argument                                                 ➢ "If I understand correctly, you're asking whether..."
    ➢ cultural expectation to interact                                      ➢ "Our current data suggests..."
    ➢ desire to contribute                                              or:
                                                                            ➢ "I do not have that information at the moment, but I would
                                                                              investigate it."
        We do not need to know everything to appear competent.
Sometimes: "I don't know.“ is more professional than inventing an answer.


   12/08/2026     Prof. Lamya Abdullah - IKHT - BINT24 - SoSe2026                                                                     25
Handling Difficult Questions



When You Don't Agree
                                                                                     Defend the idea without attacking the person.
Avoid:
  "No, that's wrong.“


Try:
  • "I see your point. Our analysis, however, suggests ..."

or:
  • "That's one possible interpretation. We approached it differently because ..."

or:
  • "Could you clarify what aspect you mean?"

Challenge: Same Message — Different Audience, cont.



A message: "Our team rejected the proposed solution because the security risks are too high.“


Possible framing:
  A. To a professor:

     "Our analysis indicates that the proposed solution introduces significant security risks.“

  B. International Project Team

     “After reviewing the proposed solution, our team identified several significant security risks. We don’t think it is the best option in
  its current form, so we suggest looking at alternative approaches together.”

  C. To a manager:
     "We recommend not proceeding with the proposed solution because the associated security risk exceeds our acceptable level.“


  D. To a client:
     "Based on our assessment, we recommend an alternative approach that provides a better security-risk balance."

A well-organized presentation keeps your audience engaged and helps them understand your use case.
Tell the Story (Only Suggestions and ideas):
  • Title & Introduction – Case title, context teaser
  • Overview – Summary, setting, goals
  • Team Composition – Size, backgrounds, roles
  • Communication Styles – Observed patterns, mismatches
  • Story – Short timeline of events
  • Challenges – 2–5 key intercultural/heterogeneous issues
  • Theory Applied – Model used to analyse (e.g., Hofstede, Belbin, GRPI, …)
  • Solutions / Strategies – What was done or proposed
  • Outcome – Results achieved, unresolved issues                                                        Presentation:
  • Lessons Learned – Main takeaways                                                                     Time: 8-9 mins => Q&A
  • Reflection & Next Steps – Recommendations
                                                                                                         Presenters: All of the Group memebers
  • References / Appendix – Sources, visuals
